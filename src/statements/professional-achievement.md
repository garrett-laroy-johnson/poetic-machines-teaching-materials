My professional work moves among three connected practices: making sound and media art, researching how people learn and create with technology, and building the open instruments, resources, and gatherings through which others can do the same. Across all three, I foreground approaches and methods over specific tools: generative and indeterminate systems, the interplay of analog and digital signals, and collective improvisation. Each practice feeds my teaching, and much of this work reaches learners well beyond my own classrooms.

## Creative Practice

My studio practice moves between real-time environments (Max/MSP/Jitter), creative coding (p5.js, Processing), digital audio workstations (Ableton Live), and embedded audio programming in C and Gen~ on Daisy Seeds, reflecting the same range of tools and movement among them that I teach.

Since 2023, my live electronics project, HYPERGRACE, combines custom Max/MSP and gen~ software, gestural controllers, and modular synthesizers that I build from kits or design myself, often spatialized for the site. In 2026, HYPERGRACE performed in 16 channels for the CLEAT series at Elastic Arts in Chicago, and on October 22–23, I will perform it in 32 channels at the third SPACEOUT International Ambisonics Festival at the University of Michigan.

My installations bring these systems to architectural scale, using generative and algorithmic methods throughout. _Post Tenebras Lux_ (Co-Prosperity Sphere, Chicago, 2025) combined custom circuit boards, Daisy Seed microcontrollers, car speakers, and custom vacuumformed plastic speaker bodies developed with a custom p5.js algorithm. The real-time generative projection used the same fluidic algorithm and patch cables between circuit-boards fed real-time control voltage to engender a constantly modulating soundscape. _difference's rainbow_ (2025), made with queer new media artist Odette Stout for _Anthropocene Consequences_ at Watershed Art and Ecology, curated by Claire Pentecost and Brian Holmes, projects real-time driftign randows composed in p5.js through sheets of organza; my accompanying essay appeared in _The Non-Standard_. _Sigil 002_, a layered acrylic sculpture embedded with speakers and topped by Daisy-based modules whose custom code sonifies a dynamical feedback system between “truth” and “beauty” modules, will be shown at the Voltaje Art and Technology Salon in Bogotá in November 2026. Other recent work includes a generative audio system for _Salish Sea_ (Carver Substation, Richmond, Virginia, 2024); _Solar Winds_ (2024), a risograph artist book whose images I generated through a custom pipeline of Stable Diffusion and computer vision in p5.js; the electronic score for an episode of Dakota Gearhart's animated series _Life Touching Life_ (SoMad, New York, 2025); and _Peace Sigil_, a 3D environment for the exhibition _The Sorcerer's Grimoire_ on New Art City (2026).

Collaboration and participation are keystones of my practice. _I Want an AI for President_ (2024), a live-action role-play created with Kristin McWharter and staged at Co-Prosperity Sphere as part of Counter-DNC programming, asks participants to self-organize their labor through AI-assisted rhetorical envisioning and democratic consensus-making, probing under what conditions people might change their mind about power and artifical intelligence. _Spectral Truths_ (Elastic Arts, 2026) is an auto-theory performance lecture with percussionist Avreayl Ra, for which I built a retro desktop emulator in p5.js that works as a performable archive of the writing and cinematic work of my late friend and queer elder, the artist and activist Leslie L Smith.

Earlier, I created responsive media for interactive dance works presented at the Kennedy Center (2016) and at the International Conference on Movement and Computing at Goldsmiths, University of London (2017), performed live electronics at the national conference of the Society for Electro-Acoustic Music in the United States (SEAMUS) and at the Contemporary Arts Center in Cincinnati, and directed the laptop orchestra LORKAS.

## Open Instruments and Community Education

I founded and direct CCAM (Center for Concrete and Abstract Machines), a Chicago 501(c)(3) for sound and media art education and production that received a 2024 Artist-Run Chicago Fund award from Hyde Park Art Center and Art Design Chicago. With CCAM's Estuary Group, a collective of musicians, technologists, researchers, and designers, I design low-cost instruments for teaching. I designed the circuit board for Estuary, a reprogrammable Eurorack module built on the Daisy Patch SM microcontroller (2024). For the 2026–27 workshops, I revised the modules, wrote new firmware to succeed the original 2025 firmware by Garnet Grimm, a CCAM community member, and designed Concrete Mixer, a four-voice stereo audio and control-voltage mixer. I also designed and wrote a zine that documents the system for workshop participants. I also designed CCAM Earth, a reprogrammable desktop synthesizer built on the Daisy Seed, and with my former student Juan Flores wrote three firmwares that let groups devise their own Chicago house performances for _Futurhythmachines: Chicago House Music_ (2024). The firmware and circuit designs for these instruments are public on GitHub. CCAM sells Estuary modules at workshops and at Experimental Sound Studio's experimental garage sale and I have and continue to work with community members as they write their own firmware.

These instruments anchor workshops in which participants learn to make electronic music through modular synthesis. Estuary workshops have taken place at Hairpin Arts Center in Chicago (2025) and the Eskenazi Museum of Art at Indiana University Bloomington (2026), with more scheduled at Public Space One in Iowa City (November 2026) and Iowa State University's Graduate Design Studio (January 2027). For the Alien Screamer workshop at Temple University's Electronics Faire (2024), I designed circuit boards from a Ray Wilson schematic and published the materials with an interactive web-based build guide; the workshop is now part of the Estuary Group's repertoire, where it serves as a way for new teachers to learn to lead it.

I also publish teaching materials openly. My course website, practices.digital, makes my syllabi, tutorials, and runnable p5.js examples freely available. I taught “Intro to Microsound,” a four-part course in sound programming with Max's gen~, at Experimental Sound Studio (2025), and since 2025 I have created _Intro to Microsound_, a YouTube tutorial series on gen~ published through CCAM with a public codebase of Max examples. In October 2026, with the learning scientist and PhD candidate Maddie Brucker and Processing Foundation executive board member Cassie Tarakajian, I organized Processing Community Day Chicago: Emergence and Improvisation at MANA Contemporary, a gathering of Chicago's creative coding community.

## Research, Writing, and Pedagogical Scholarship

My research connects responsive media, collective improvisation, and learning. My dissertation, _Diagrammatic Media: Subjectivity, Ecology, and Event_ (Arizona State University, 2022), was among the top-rated 2023 LABS submissions, and its abstract was published in _Leonardo_ (2024). I have published in the _Journal of Somaesthetics_ (2019) and _Reconceptualizing Educational Research Methodology_ (2022), in the proceedings of the International Conference on Movement and Computing (2018, 2019) and HCI International (2015), and in edited volumes from Edinburgh University Press (_Rhythm and Critique_, 2022) and 284 Publishers (_The Anthropocene Laboratory_, 2024). Two chapters on art and artificial intelligence, one co-written with the artist Eva Davidova, are forthcoming in _AI Halftime: Multispecies Ecologies of Emerging Cognitions_. I was a postdoctoral researcher with Leonardo/ISAST in 2024–2025, and I have given more than thirty conference talks, along with invited talks at the Audio Engineering Society (Shure headquarters), New York University's Integrated Design & Media program, Virginia Commonwealth University's Department of Kinetic Imaging, HDK-Valand in Gothenburg, the Center for Process Studies, Arts, Letters & Numbers, and Concordia University's Milieux Institute.

Much of my research addresses teaching and learning directly. I co-founded and worked as a researcher on a National Science Foundation–funded research–practice partnership, “Engaging Teachers and Neurodiverse Middle School Students in Tangible and Creative Computational Thinking Activities” (2021–2025), which co-designed music-based, embodied computational thinking activities with middle school teachers. With collaborators from this work, I co-authored “From Technique to Technicity: Explorations of Chairs, Neurodiversity, and Schooling” (2022) and co-presented on wearable technologies for active learning and sensory regulation at the Arizona Council on Exceptional Children (2022).

## Curation, Organizing, and Service to the Field

I have organized more than a dozen scholarly and artistic gatherings, which I approach not as mere event planning but as the creative design of learning communities. I co-organized the exhibition _How to See in the Dark_ (Co-Prosperity Sphere, Chicago, 2025), supported by a competitive grant, and co-convened its symposium with Muindi Fanuel Muindi; my letters of support helped the New York–based media artists Dakota Gearhart and Eva Davidova receive Foundation for Contemporary Arts Emergency Grants to install their work and attend. Through CCAM, I have co-organized three annual editions (2024–2026) of _We Don't Know Yet: What a Cinema Can Do_, an expanded cinema night with the Onion City Film Festival that centers BIPOC and queer artists, and _Futurhythmachines: Chicago House Music_ (2024), a panel with Thomas DeFrantz, Meida McNeal, and Duane Powell alongside workshops and performances. CCAM also provides organizational support to BADS_Lab: Black Arts and Decolonial Sciences, a lab and fellowship program in Chicago and São Paulo. My curatorial residency at Elastic Arts, _Twirls and Swirls_, begins December 9, 2026, with a performance and conversation with The Dutchess Gemini, a Black trans drag performer and media artist. This four-evening series of performances, screenings, and conversations with queer artists will also serve as release parties for zines I will make and design based on my salon-style conversations with the artists.

Earlier, I organized the _(in)formal (in)formations_ symposium at NEW INC in New York (2023) and four Posthuman Network symposia at Arizona State University (2017–2020). For the International Conference on Movement and Computing (MOCO), I chaired the doctoral consortium (2019) and the online SloMoCo series (2021), served as virtual events chair (2022), and have served on the steering committee since 2020. I have reviewed for _Leonardo_, NIME, and MOCO. At SAIC, I served on the Art and Technology / Sound Practices curriculum committee and graduate admissions committee (2023–2024).

## Plans at UC Santa Cruz

At UCSC, I will continue to perform and exhibit spatial and site-specific sound work, and I would welcome the chance to contribute to the April in Santa Cruz Festival of Creative Music and the Pacific Rim Music Festival. I will expand the Estuary instruments, already public on GitHub, and their workshop materials into open resources that Creative Technologies and Music students can build, modify, and carry into their own communities. I will also study and publish on neurodivergence-responsive online teaching of creative coding, drawing on Erin Manning's work on neurodiversity and Alfred North Whitehead's philosophy of education and contributing to the scholarship of teaching that the Teaching Professor series values. I look forward to collaborating with the Department of Performance, Play, and Design on participatory and game-based work like _I Want an AI for President_, and across the Arts Division on critical approaches to AI, sound and listening, and aural diversity.
