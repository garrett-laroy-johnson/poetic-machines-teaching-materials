#### Coding Sketches (18%)

Most weeks you will complete a short coding sketch that practices that week's technique; each one is described on its week's page. Sketches are graded on effort: a complete, good-faith attempt earns full credit, even if it doesn't fully work. Bring your questions to class, where we share and discuss sketches each week.

#### Group Presentation (18%)

In groups of 5–6, you will present on an area of creative coding and digital art outside what we make in p5.js: **generative art**, **sound and music**, or **games and simulation**. Each student chooses one artist from the group's topic (artist lists are on the Group Presentation page) and presents for 4–5 minutes:

- show one or two of the artist's works and explain how they relate to the topic, paying particular attention to how the artist uses code and other technology
- read and discuss the artist's own description of the work
- give your own response: what you see, how it makes you feel, what you think it's about
- describe how your own interests intersect with the topic, and how you might pursue it further

#### Participation (10%)

As the UIC Department of Art describes, a studio course is built on active participation: making together, thinking together, and learning from one another. Participation means coming to class prepared, sharing your work in progress, asking questions, and engaging thoughtfully in critique of your classmates' work. You can participate by speaking, in the Zoom chat, by messaging me privately, or in written responses to classmates' work.

#### Late Work

Coding sketches may be submitted up to one week after the end of their module for half credit; after that, they receive no credit. Projects may be submitted late with a penalty of one letter grade for each week they are late.
