Each week pairs the technique we are learning with music that uses it: experimental electronic music, drone and ambient, pop, and video game music. Listen closely: what is the sound source, how does it change over time, and what would you need to build it in Max?

**Module 1: Synthesizing Drones + Ambient Textures**

- *Week 3, additive synthesis and waveforms:* [Intro to Spectral Music](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rs7b4WSxF3w); Giacinto Scelsi, [*Konx-Om-Pax*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BnZQVRmJObw) (1969); Konx-om-Pax, [*Regional Surrealism*](https://konx-om-pax.bandcamp.com/album/regional-surrealism) (2012); Kali Malone, *The Sacrificial Code* (2019), drones for pipe organ; Booker T. & the M.G.'s, “Green Onions” (1962), whose Hammond organ builds tones from drawbar harmonics, a mechanical form of additive synthesis; Koji Kondo, *Super Mario Bros.* (1985), written for the NES's pulse, triangle, and noise channels
- *Week 4, subtractive synthesis, filters, amplitude modulation, LFOs:* Pauline Oliveros, *Bye Bye Butterfly* (1965); Wendy Carlos, *Switched-On Bach* (1968), made on the Moog modular; Daft Punk, “One More Time” (2000), and the filter sweeps of French house; Yuzo Koshiro, *Streets of Rage 2* (1992), modulation synthesis on the Sega Genesis sound chip
- *Week 5, envelopes and notes:* Suzanne Ciani, *Seven Waves* (1982); Éliane Radigue, *Trilogie de la Mort* (1998); Laraaji, *Ambient 3: Day of Radiance* (1980); Hiroshi Yoshimura, *Music for Nine Post Cards* (1982)
- *Week 6, sequencing:* Morton Subotnick, *Silver Apples of the Moon* (1967), made on the Buchla modular; Donna Summer and Giorgio Moroder, “I Feel Love” (1977); Kraftwerk, “Trans-Europe Express” (1977); Phuture, “Acid Tracks” (1987), from Chicago's acid house
- *Week 7, keyboard and mouse as instruments; generative music:* Laurie Spiegel, *Music Mouse* (1986), software played with the computer mouse; Brian Eno, *Music for Airports* (1978); Brian Eno's generative score for the video game *Spore* (2008)

**Module 2: Working with Recorded and Live Sound**

- *Week 9, recording and sample playback:* Pierre Schaeffer, *Étude aux chemins de fer* (1948); Alvin Lucier, *I Am Sitting in a Room* (1969); Delia Derbyshire, *Doctor Who* theme (1963); Daft Punk, “Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger” (2001), built on a sample of Edwin Birdsong's “Cola Bottle Baby”; J Dilla, *Donuts* (2006)
- *Week 10, game controllers as interfaces:* Michel Waisvisz, *The Hands*; Laetitia Sonami, the *lady's glove*; Imogen Heap and the MI.MU gloves; game soundtracks that respond to play: Disasterpeace, *Fez* (2012); Lena Raine, *Celeste* (2018)
- *Week 11, camera as interface:* David Rokeby, *Very Nervous System* (1986–)
- *Weeks 12–15, generative systems, IDM, and techno:* 65daysofstatic, *No Man's Sky: Music for an Infinite Universe* (2016), procedural game music; Aphex Twin, *Selected Ambient Works 85–92* (1992); Autechre, *Amber* (1994); Drexciya, *Neptune's Lair* (1999); Underground Resistance and Jeff Mills, Detroit techno
