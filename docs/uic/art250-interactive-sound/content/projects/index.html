UIC | ART 250 | Introduction to Interactive Sound

Key Concepts:

You will earn most of your grade through three pieces of work. All are presented for collective critique, and the Midterm and Final Project are assessed with the course project rubric below.

Print Poems (10%)

Before we make any sound, we make poems. In Week 2, use Max to compose text by rule and chance and print it to the Max console with [print]. We look to Mozart's musical dice game, William S. Burroughs's cut-up technique, and Judy Malloy's Uncle Roger as models of writing with procedures.

requirements

  • generate text from a vocabulary you choose, stored in messages or lists (zl objects)
  • use at least one source of chance ([random] or [drunk])
  • use timing or counting ([metro], [counter]) to give the poem a structure

submission

Your .maxpat file and a copy or screenshot of a poem it produced. Print Poems are graded on completion and good-faith experimentation.

Midterm: Synthesis Performance or Generative Sound Piece (30%)

Drawing on Module 1, Synthesizing Drones + Ambient Textures, build a Max patch that either you perform live or that performs itself, for 3–5 minutes. Think of the drone and ambient listenings: how a sound can unfold slowly, how texture can be a form.

Choose one path:

  • Performance: you play the patch live, using keyboard and mouse controls ([key], [mousestate], [kslider], sliders) to shape the sound in real time.
  • Generative: the patch unfolds on its own through timing, chance, and sequencing; you start it and let it play.

requirements

  • built from the objects and techniques we have covered, so that you can explain every part of your patch
  • at least two Module 1 synthesis techniques: additive synthesis, subtractive synthesis and filtering, amplitude modulation, or LFO modulation
  • envelopes ([adsr~], [line~], or [function]) shaping at least one sound
  • a timing or sequencing structure ([metro] and [counter], [matrixctrl], or [preset])
  • performance path: at least two real-time controls mapped from the keyboard or mouse; generative path: a piece that changes meaningfully over 3–5 minutes without your intervention
  • a clearly labeled patch with a master level control ([live.gain~])

submission

Your .maxpat file(s) and an audio or screen recording of the piece. Present your piece at Midterm Presentations (Week 8).

Final Project (40%)

Create a performance, generative sound system, sound sculpture, or interactive installation that builds on Module 2, Working with Recorded and Live Sound, and brings in what you learned in Module 1.

requirements

  • sample playback ([buffer~], [groove~], or [playlist~]) using sounds you recorded or sourced, with sources credited
  • at least one gestural or sensing interface: game controller, camera, keyboard and mouse, or another sensor
  • at least one Module 1 synthesis or modulation technique
  • a proposal (Week 13) describing your concept, technical plan, and equipment needs

submission

Your patch(es), sound files, and documentation (audio or video recording, plus photos for sculptures and installations). Perform or install your work for Final Project critiques (Week 15).
